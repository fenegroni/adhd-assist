# Profile

*This is an example. Replace it with your own — a short true profile beats a long invented one.*

The idea: **you write this when you are well, and the skill reads it back to you when you are
not.** It is not advice from an assistant. It is your own judgment, returned at the moment yours
is least available. That only works if the content is genuinely yours, so treat everything below
as a starting point to overwrite rather than something to keep.

Lives in Google Drive as `adhd-assist/profile.md`.

## The menu

The things the skill is allowed to suggest, ranked by how little activation energy they need —
because when you're stuck, the cheapest option is the only one that will actually happen.

| # | Option | Cost to start | Best for |
|---|--------|---------------|----------|
| 0 | Eat something | near zero | flat, wired-tired, irritable, "productive day" |
| 1 | Rest somewhere comfortable | near zero | flat, overwhelmed, wired-tired |
| 2 | Breathing | near zero | wired, spiky, post-frustration |
| 3 | Music, or something absorbing | near zero | under-stimulated, can't start |
| 4 | 5 minutes of movement | near zero | restless, flat, scattered |
| 5 | 15 minutes of real exercise | low | restless, flat, under-stimulated |
| 6 | Go outside | low | locked, scattered, restless |
| 7 | Stop for the day | low | hyperfocus-locked, circling |
| 8 | Meditation | medium | overwhelmed, too many threads |

Medication is deliberately **not** on this menu. If you take it, it lives in `medication.md` and
is checked before the menu is reached. If you don't, there is no `medication.md` and medication
is never raised at all.

**Food is rank zero.** Check whether the real answer is just food before matching any state —
especially late in the day, or on a day you'd describe as productive. If you take stimulants,
this is stronger than it sounds: they suppress appetite, so "I was really productive today" and
"I haven't eaten today" are not two facts, they're the same fact, and the hunger arrives in the
evening looking exactly like a mood.

**Cheap beats correct.** A five-minute thing that actually happens beats the right thing that
doesn't. Rank by cost to start, not by how much good it would do.

**External obligations override state.** `[Name yours here.]` If something in your life has a
deadline that doesn't care how you feel, it wins over rest when it's owed — that is what makes
it different from every other item on the list. Say how long it can slip before it stops being
one option among several and becomes the only answer.

## The fixed point

`[Name the one thing in your day that does not move. For most people this is bedtime.]`

Evenings are the usual crunch: something long left to do, food not eaten, and a fixed end time.
They don't all fit. Decide *now*, while you're well, which one gives — because in the moment
you'll give up the wrong one.

The worked example, with bedtime as the fixed point:

- **The bedtime holds.** Work backwards from it, not forwards from whenever things started.
- **If the long thing starts late, shorten it.** That is what gives.
- **Eat before it if there's time to digest** — roughly an hour.
- **If it's imminent, something quick and sugary instead**, then cut the long thing short and
  eat properly afterwards.
- **Dizziness or shakiness on an empty stomach is food, not a state.** Sit down, then carbs.
  Don't reach into the menu for anything else.

## States

The patterns worth naming. Each is a label you can also just say directly ("I'm scattered") to
skip the diagnosis step entirely.

**If none of them fit, don't force one.** Say what you actually saw, or say nothing. A confident
match to the wrong state is worse than no match at all.

Fill in each "Looks like" **in your own words, describing your writing** — messages are most of
what the skill can actually see.

**Scattered.** Can't start. The task is clear, the cost of beginning feels enormous.
Looks like: `[many small questions, none followed through, nothing committed to]`

**Locked.** Hyper-focused and can't stop. Usually late, usually productive-looking and not
actually productive.
Looks like: `[long sessions, escalating scope, the ask keeps growing]`

**Wired-tired.** Depleted but not settled. Often the tail end of a day.
Looks like: `[short clipped messages, irritation at small things, wanting a result immediately]`

**Under-stimulated.** Restless, bored, reaching for novelty.
Looks like: `[starting something new instead of finishing, tangents, "actually, what if"]`

**Overwhelmed.** Too many threads open, none closable.
Looks like: `[scope inflation, five things in one message, "I need to figure out"]`

**Flat.** Low drive. Not sad necessarily, just nothing pulling.
Looks like: `[your words — this one is hard to spot and worth getting right]`

## What the skill can actually see

Being honest about this matters, because a suggestion built on a signal it doesn't have is noise.

**Can see:** the time and day. How long since you last wrote. Message length, and whether it's
composed or fragmentary. Typo density. Circling the same point. Threads opened and abandoned.
Repeat questions. Scope inflation. Frustration in the words.

**Can see, via calendar:** `[whatever you already keep there. Recurring series tell it little;
individual events carry an updated timestamp each time you move one, so repeatedly pushing
something back is visible — and that is often the strongest signal available.]`

**Cannot see:** whether you ate or slept. Whether you did the thing or only moved the block.
Whether you took a dose. Anything at all between conversations. Whether the last suggestion
worked, unless you say so or it's inferable from how you turn up next.

## Feedback

The cheapest possible loop. Nothing to rate.

- If a suggestion lands, you don't have to say so — it gets inferred from you going quiet and
  coming back steadier.
- If it doesn't land, "no" or "didn't work" or ignoring it all count as data.
- If the read was wrong, **"wrong" is a complete sentence.** It's the most useful thing you can
  give it and it costs one word.

The log is the record of what works. Nothing gets summarised back into this file.
